# How the site is organised

Three levels, always: the hub, a space, a tool.

=> Hub (all spaces) | Space (one department) | Tool (one page of work)

## The three levels
- **Hub**: the home page. One tile per space you were given.
- **Space**: one department. One tile per tool you were given.
- **Tool**: one page under the site's bar. The bar belongs to the site; everything below it is the tool.
- Every space and tool has an **id** (small letters, digits, dashes). It is permanent and never used again, even after a delete.

## Four roles
| Role | What it does |
|---|---|
| IT admin | Lets people in, makes spaces, names their admins, sets the site rules, reads the audit. On the IT admin page a space is a tile; it is changed or deleted only in the window the tile opens |
| Space admin | Makes the tools of the space, names their managers, adds people, gives access |
| Tool manager | Builds and looks after one tool: its page, its tables, its usage guide, its access |
| Member | Uses the tools he was given |

## Four levels on a tool
| Level | The person can |
|---|---|
| Read | See what is there |
| Contribute | Add rows and change his own |
| Edit | Change anyone's rows |
| Approve | Also delete rows |

## How a person gets a tool
- By name: the manager puts him on the tool's access list with a level.
- Or with the space: a tool can be "open to everyone in the space" at one level.
- A manager always has the top level on his tool.

## Two things that surprise people
- Being **IT admin does not open a department's data**. To look inside a space, IT must name itself its admin, and that is written in the audit.
- The pages only show and hide buttons. **The database decides** every single action again. A hidden button that someone finds a way to press is still refused.
