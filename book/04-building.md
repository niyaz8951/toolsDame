# Building a tool

A tool is one page. The site gives it the look, the bar, the sign-in, the people and the database. The builder brings only what the tool does.

=> Describe | Give the kit to the AI | Paste the answer | Check | Preview | Make live

## Step by step
1. The space admin creates the tile: a name, an id, a colour, who gets it.
2. On the tool's admin page the builder chooses **one of two ways**: build a new page, or bring a page he already has.
3. **Build a new page**: he writes in his own words what the tool should do, and answers one question: does it save data in the database?
4. He copies the **starter kit** and pastes it into the approved AI. The kit is one text: the rules of the site, the building blocks, a starting page, and his description.
5. He pastes the AI's answer back. The site **checks** it and says in plain words what is wrong, with a ready "fix request" for the AI: one complete text with the points, every rule of the site, the database rules and the page itself.
6. He uploads. The upload becomes the **preview**: only managers see it. He tries it.
7. He presses **Make live** twice. Now everyone with the tool gets it.

The site shows one step at a time. The next step appears only when this one is done.

## What the check looks for
- Anything loaded from the internet, or a call to a server.
- Its own header, sign-in, fonts or colours.
- Browser storage, background workers, a password box.
- A table the page uses but does not describe.

## Versions
- Every upload is a new version and is never changed afterwards.
- **Roll back** brings an older version back at once.
- A stored page runs only if its fingerprint still equals the one recorded at upload.

## Helping the people who use it
- **Usage guide**: the manager writes a few steps. People get them behind the information button of the tool. With no guide written, the site writes a short one itself.
- Until a tool is live, its information button carries a red dot and explains everything about building.

## What a builder never needs
> No repository, no hosting, no SQL, no key, no IT ticket. And no real names, prices or customer data go into the AI: the kit contains only the site's public rules and the builder's own description.
